1. What the project claims versus what the code does

Summary of findings

The project is not small, but every AI behaviour in it is a network call to a third-party hosted model. There is no model in the repository that was trained, tuned or evaluated by the team. That is the substantive weakness, and it is fixable.

Immediate security action, independent of any roadmap decision

The Gemini API key ships inside the client-side JavaScript bundle and is readable by anyone who loads the site. All model calls should be moved behind the Express server and the key rotated. The same applies to any other credentials committed to configuration files.

The regulatory constraint

Under EU MDR Rule 11, software that outputs a differential diagnosis or a triage decision is classified as a Class IIa medical device, and a disclaimer does not change the intended purpose. Competing on diagnostic accuracy therefore walks the project into a regulated category it cannot ship in. Competing on verifiability does not.

Reposition the project in one sentence

Not "an AI health assistant" — those exist. Instead: a health AI that can prove every statement it makes, ask the questions that actually reduce uncertainty, and report its own failure rate. Anyone can call a language model. Almost nobody builds the verification layer around it, and that layer is measurable, defensible and squarely an AI systems contribution.

What this means strategically

HealthPlix is not a competitor to a patient assistant; it is a different product class serving the doctor. The comparison is best read as an observation about where clinical value sits, not as a claim that the project already exists. Competing on ambient scribing against 100 million consultations of proprietary training data is not viable. Competing where they have no presence is.

The structural advantage available to a patient-side product

An EMR vendor only ever observes what happens inside its own software. An Indian patient typically consults three or four doctors across unconnected systems, takes medicines that nobody reconciles, and spends 364 days a year outside a clinic. Only the patient holds the complete record. A patient-side application can therefore build things that are structurally impossible for an EMR vendor, regardless of how much capital or data that vendor has.

6. Features: before the visit

Window: booking confirmed to walking in, typically hours to days. Currently the entire state of the art is a reminder notification. The patient is at home, unhurried and willing to answer questions; the doctor, who will have seven minutes, is not.

Vitals from the phone camera (remote photoplethysmography)looks 10/10 · 3 weeks

The patient looks into the front camera for 30 seconds and the application estimates heart rate and respiratory rate from micro-variations in facial skin colour caused by blood flow.

Why nobody has it

It sounds as though it cannot work, so no product in the Indian consumer market ships it. The underlying signal-processing approach is well established and published, and it requires no hardware the patient does not already own.

How you build it

Detect the face, average the green channel over a forehead region across frames, detrend and bandpass the resulting one-dimensional signal to 0.7–4 Hz, then take the dominant FFT peak. The published CHROM and POS methods are a few hundred lines. Validate against an inexpensive pulse oximeter.

Stack

OpenCV or MediaPipe face mesh, SciPy signal processing, FFT

Come-prepared engine: predict the tests the doctor will orderlooks 7/10 · 2 weeks

From the intake conversation, predict the laboratory tests most likely to be ordered and advise the patient to complete them beforehand, or to arrive fasting.

Why nobody has it

Possibly the highest practical-value item on this list for Indian patients, and entirely absent from the market. At present a patient travels to a clinic, is told to return fasting, and loses a second day and a second consultation fee.

How you build it

Retrieve the workup section of the relevant guideline for the presenting complaint and extract the recommended investigations. A retrieval and extraction task over a corpus already being built, not a prediction model.

Guideline retrieval, structured extraction, fasting-rule table

7. Features: between visits

Window: day two to day sixty, by far the longest. Currently only fixed-schedule medication reminders, sent identically to everyone, with no observation of whether anything worked. This is where deterioration, side effects and silent non-adherence all occur unobserved, and it is the only window long enough to produce time-series data.

Passive digital phenotyping from app interactionlooks 10/10 · 2 weeks

Infer trends in wellbeing from how the patient uses the application rather than what they report: typing speed, response latency, session times, message length and sentiment drift.

Why nobody has it

An active research area under the name digital phenotyping, and no Indian consumer health product implements it. The notable point is that the raw material is already being logged.

How you build it

Firestore messages already carry timestamps. Derive per-session features, establish a personal baseline, then apply trend and change-point detection. No new data collection is required.

Stack

Feature engineering over existing logs, Ruptures or CUSUM, sentiment model

Drug side-effect early warninglooks 9/10 · 2 weeks

When a new symptom is reported, check it against the known adverse-effect profile of every drug the patient recently started and flag the probable culprit.

Why nobody has it

Interaction checkers run at the moment of prescribing. Nobody watches for the effect appearing two weeks later, which is precisely when patients discontinue medication silently and no clinician finds out.

How you build it

SIDER is an open drug side-effect dataset. Match reported symptoms against the profiles of active drugs, weighted by time since initiation. Predominantly a data-joining problem.

Stack

SIDER dataset, RxNorm normalisation, temporal scoring

8. Features: after the visit

Window: outcome and continuity, day seven onward. Currently nothing — no platform in this market systematically asks whether the treatment worked. Outcome data is the one asset that compounds, and no incumbent is collecting it because they lose the patient at the clinic door.

Causal treatment effectiveness across your own userslooks 10/10 · 3 weeks

For patients with similar presentations, how long did symptoms actually take to resolve under each treatment approach, adjusted so that sicker patients do not make effective treatments appear worse.

Why nobody has it

This is the data flywheel absent from the market, because nobody collects patient-reported outcomes after the visit. Naive averages would be misleading, and correcting for that confounding is the substantive contribution.

How you build it

Propensity score matching or inverse probability weighting over the outcome table. DoWhy and EconML implement the estimators; the work is defining treatment, outcome and confounders honestly and stating the limitations explicitly.

Stack

DoWhy or EconML, propensity matching, outcome schema

Patient-reported outcome dashboard for the doctorlooks 8/10 · 2 weeks

Shows the doctor what happened after their patients left: of the last fifty with a given presentation, what proportion reported resolution by day seven.

Why nobody has it

Doctors almost never receive outcome feedback. EMR analytics report volume and revenue, not recovery, because the EMR loses the patient at the door. This is also the feature that drives doctor-side adoption of the application.

How you build it

Aggregate follow-up responses per presentation. The difficult part is raising follow-up response rates, which the proactive check-in agent addresses.

Stack

Aggregation over outcome table, doctor-side view, consent model

11. Positioning and sources

The positioning statement

"The industry has optimised the seven minutes a patient spends with a doctor. We instrument the hundred and fifty days around it. We collect a structured baseline before the visit, including vitals measured through the phone camera. We watch for side effects and deterioration against that patient's own baseline rather than a population range. We reconcile prescriptions across every doctor they see, which an EMR vendor structurally cannot do because it only sees its own network. And we are the only ones who ask afterwards whether the treatment actually worked. Every statement the system makes is audited against retrieved clinical guidelines, and we report our own unsupported-claim rate — a number no consumer health product publishes. It is not a competing EMR. It is the patient-side intelligence layer that no EMR can build."

Terminology to avoid

Do not describe the project as a symptom checker or a chatbot. Both terms map it onto a commoditised category in the listener's mind before the sentence finishes. Lead with simulation, reconciliation and verification instead.
